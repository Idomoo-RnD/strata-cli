# Research basis and source map

> Provenance for the five editorial files. Read it when a rule here seems to conflict with a
> measured fact elsewhere in this skill - **the measured fact wins**, and this file says where
> the editorial rule came from so the conflict can be resolved rather than averaged.

Research snapshot: 2026-08-23.

This skill synthesizes established editing grammar, empirical viewer-attention research, direct interviews with working editors, factual-media standards, platform guidance, documentary ethics frameworks, and accessibility standards. The sources support decisions; they are not a list of fixed formulas.

## How to use the evidence

- **Editing grammar sources** define common tools and vocabulary.
- **Empirical studies** explain viewer orientation and attention under specific test conditions; they do not prove that one creative style is universally better.
- **Editor interviews** are primary craft accounts from individual productions. Treat them as situated evidence, not genre law.
- **News and documentary frameworks** establish stronger integrity boundaries than fictional or promotional editing.
- **Platform research** is provider-published and often advertising-focused. Use it as a placement starting point, verify current specifications, and test with the actual audience.
- **Accessibility standards** can impose requirements and should shape the edit early.

## Evidence-to-practice synthesis

| Research finding | Editorial implication in this skill |
|---|---|
| Continuity edits reduce the effort needed to map time, space, and action, and are often less consciously noticed. | Use eye trace, action match, eyelines, and geography when immersion or rapid comprehension matters. Break continuity only for a defined experience. |
| Altered/discontinuous joins can increase perceptual response and reorientation. | Discontinuity is an expressive cost/benefit choice, not free energy. Reduce competing complexity and preserve an anchor. |
| Shot length alone does not define perceived pace; modern films show broader changes in motion, luminance, framing, and shot patterns. | Diagnose information, performance, movement, sound, and contrast before prescribing faster cuts or an average shot length. |
| Professional editors repeatedly describe performance, emotion, context, and sound as pace determinants. | Build the dramatic or factual backbone before polish and let reactions, silence, and consequences determine timing. |
| Comedy relies on take choice, timing, and the ability to experience material freshly; reactions can be decisive. | Protect setup/reveal/reaction order and evaluate in continuous playback rather than trimming every pause automatically. |
| Horror suspense may work by extending shots slightly beyond comfortable duration. | Do not equate horror with rapid montage. Control offscreen space, waiting, rupture, and aftermath. |
| Action and sports editors build in layers around story, tent-pole events, participant choices, and crowd/reaction context. | Establish goals and causality first; add detail, rhythm, and spectacle after the audience can follow the event. |
| Documentary editors often discover or write structure in post, while archive and interview should interact rather than duplicate each other. | Log deeply, maintain a factual chronology and paper edit, and prevent archive from becoming wallpaper. |
| Factual standards prohibit edits that materially mislead, alter quotation meaning, or present generic/altered imagery as event evidence. | Truth overrides dramatic convenience; provenance, context, reaction chronology, and sound authenticity require explicit audits. |
| Animation editors build performance and timing in story reels before costly final animation. | Prove story, dialogue, pose timing, effects, and music relationships in animatics and review in full context. |
| Major short-form platforms emphasize native vertical framing, safe zones, early relevance, sound, text/context, and testing. | Rebuild platform variants rather than center-cropping masters; use hooks as honest promises and avoid nonstop empty stimulation. |
| WCAG requires captions for applicable prerecorded synchronized web media, audio description or alternatives in specified cases, and flash safety thresholds. | Plan captions, description, readable graphics, and flash checks during editorial, not only at export. |

## Foundations: cut types, continuity, split edits, and attention

- [Adobe: Cuts in film](https://www.adobe.com/creativecloud/video/post-production/cuts-in-film.html) — straight, jump, match, smash, cross-cut, and split-edit definitions; connects cuts to pacing and narrative flow.
- [Adobe: Continuity editing](https://www.adobe.com/creativecloud/video/hub/ideas/what-is-continuity-editing-in-film.html) — eyeline matching, 180-degree rule, match on action, sound bridges, and eye trace.
- [Adobe: J-cuts and L-cuts](https://www.adobe.com/uk/creativecloud/video/discover/j-cut-and-l-cut.html) — picture/sound overlap for flow, anticipation, reaction, and montage.
- [Adobe: Video editing basics and the Rule of Six](https://www.adobe.com/in/creativecloud/video/discover/edit-a-video.html) — emotion, story, rhythm, eye trace, screen plane, and space as a cut hierarchy.
- [Smith & Henderson: Edit Blindness](https://bop.unibe.ch/JEMR/article/view/2264) — eye-tracking evidence that continuity-conforming edits can be less consciously noticed; edits coinciding with sudden motion were especially likely to pass unnoticed in the study.
- [Magliano & Zacks: The Impact of Continuity Editing in Narrative Film on Event Segmentation](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3208769/) — continuity editing supports mapping across perceptual and conceptual boundaries in narrative events.
- [Swenberg & Eriksson: Effects of Continuity or Discontinuity in Actual Film Editing](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0276237417744590) — altered joins produced increased saccade frequency and relative pupil size in the tested documentary sequence.
- [Cutting, DeLong & Nothelfer: Quicker, faster, darker](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23145246/) — historical analysis of changes in Hollywood shot length, motion, and luminance; supports treating pace as multi-variable.
- [Bruckert et al.: Where to look at the movies](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36002630/) — links editing choices and spectator gaze distributions.

## Direct editor accounts: genre, performance, rhythm, and sound

- [Art of the Cut: The Drama](https://borisfx.com/blog/aotc/art-of-the-cut-the-drama/) — performance calibration, nonlinear memory, "speed of thought," and contrast between frenetic passages and long uncomfortable holds.
- [Art of the Cut: Hacks](https://borisfx.com/blog/aotc/art-of-the-cut-hacks/) — comedy timing, take retrieval, multicamera work, and reviewing with a fresh audience perspective.
- [Adobe: Five editing tips from SNL editor Adam Epstein](https://blog.adobe.com/en/publish/2021/01/05/five-editing-tips-adam-epstein-learned-from-his-time-at-snl) — joke timing, sound precision, and editorial organization under short deadlines.
- [Art of the Cut: Alien: Romulus](https://borisfx.com/blog/aotc/art-of-the-cut-alien-romulus/) — editor Jake Roberts describes suspense as often slower than ordinary scene timing, with duration pushed beyond comfort.
- [Art of the Cut: F1](https://borisfx.com/blog/aotc/art-of-the-cut-f1/) — contextual pace, layered construction of complex races, story-first selects, and performance within spectacle.
- [Art of the Cut: Welcome to Wrexham](https://borisfx.com/blog/aotc/art-of-the-cut-welcome-to-wrexham/) — sports tent poles, uninterrupted play, event emotion, found sound, replay/slow-motion choices, and truth constraints around reaction placement.
- [Art of the Cut: Beckham](https://borisfx.com/blog/aotc/art-of-the-cut-beckham/) — deep archive logging, interview/archive dialogue, natural-sound punctuation, chronological responsibility, and documentary manipulation boundaries.
- [Art of the Cut: Him](https://borisfx.com/blog/aotc/art-of-the-cut-him/) — an editor's warning that cutting every montage beat can become staccato and monotonous; advocates finding internal picture/performance rhythm before music controls the cut.
- [Art of the Cut: Blitz](https://borisfx.com/blog/aotc/art-of-the-cut-blitz/) — sound-built montage, music restraint, handheld energy, and the risk of over-refining or overcutting rough material.
- [Art of the Cut: Tár / Monika Willi](https://borisfx.com/blog/aotc/art-of-the-cut-with-vienna-based-editor-monika-willi/) — deliberate editing, strategic impact cuts, reaction-centered scenes, and controlled information withholding.
- [Art of the Cut: Carlos](https://borisfx.com/blog/aotc/art-of-the-cut-carlos/) — music-documentary pacing as a cohesive ebb and flow across interviews, lyrics, instrumental passages, quiet, and eruption.

## Documentary craft, accountability, archive, and rights

- [BFI: In praise of the undersung art of documentary editing](https://www.bfi.org.uk/features/praise-undersung-art-documentary-editing) — documentary structure and character are often found or written in the edit without a scripted blueprint.
- [Documentary Accountability Working Group: From Reflection to Release](https://www.docaccountability.org/) — participant-centered framework built around consent, transparency, care, safety, power, and accountability.
- [International Documentary Association: Ethics of archival use](https://www.documentary.org/online-feature/ethics-archival-use-roundtable-discussion) — primary practitioner discussion of implication, context, source material, and the ethics of archival construction.
- [International Documentary Association: Archive vérité](https://documentary.org/feature/archive-verite-editing-art-form) — archive used as scene and primary storytelling material rather than as illustrative background.
- [International Documentary Association: The human factor in archival profiles](https://www.documentary.org/feature/human-factor-how-makers-profile-documentaries-shape-their-subject-archival-footage) — archive's original authorship, licensing, research, and the principle that footage should serve rather than manufacture truth.
- [International Documentary Association: Documentary editors discuss their craft](https://www.documentary.org/blog/shaping-reality-documentary-editors-discuss-their-craft-doc-u) — distillation, factual timeline, character, and story discovery across historical, vérité, and reality work.
- [International Documentary Association: Practical guide to fair use](https://documentary.org/feature/clip-show-practical-guide-fair-use) — illustrates source logging and early legal review; not a substitute for legal advice or jurisdiction-specific clearance.

## Animation and story reels

- [Pixar in a Box / Khan Academy: Storyreels](https://www.khanacademy.org/computing/pixar/storytelling/storyboard-your-film/v/pitching4) — Pixar editors explain timing boards with dialogue, effects, music, and character thought; emphasizes repeated full-film screening before final animation.
- [Making the Cut at Pixar: The Art of Editing Animation](https://www.makingthecutatpixar.com/) — companion site to research and interviews on Pixar editorial, story development, and animation production.
- [Adobe: Editing and animation of The Lady and the Dale](https://blog.adobe.com/en/publish/2021/03/05/the-editing-and-animation-of-the-lady-and-the-dale) — paper/radio edit, rough collaged boards, animation/editorial iteration, and nonliteral visual flow.

## News and factual broadcast standards

- [Associated Press: Telling the Story](https://www.ap.org/about/news-values-and-principles/telling-the-story/) — permits restrained technical correction but prohibits altering image content or quotation meaning; requires care with generic video, natural sound, and internal soundbite edits.
- [Reuters Journalistic Standards](https://reutersagency.com/about/standards-values/) — accuracy, impartiality, freedom from bias, vigilance against manipulation, and controlled use of editing/AI tools.
- [BBC Editorial Guidelines: Accuracy](https://www.bbc.co.uk/editorialguidelines/guidelines/accuracy/guidelines) — commentary and editing must not create a materially misleading impression; reconstructions require identification.
- [Ofcom Broadcasting Code: Due impartiality and due accuracy](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/tv-radio-and-on-demand/broadcast-standards/section-five-due-impartiality-accuracy) — official UK broadcast requirements for news accuracy and impartiality.

## Advertising, social, and platform-native video

- [Google/Ipsos: ABCDs of effective video creative](https://www.thinkwithgoogle.com/_qs/documents/18468/ABCDs_PDFPlaybook_April2022_Final_1mSQVej.pdf) — attention, branding, connection, and direction, refined by campaign objective.

- [YouTube Help: Search and discovery tips for Shorts](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/11914225?co=YOUTUBE._YTVideoType%3Dshorts&hl=en) — the system does not favor one format categorically; audience response and personalization matter, supporting testing over universal formulas.
- [Meta for Business: Reels ads](https://www.facebook.com/business/ads/facebook-instagram-reels-ads) — official guidance and provider-run test results for vertical 9:16, audio, safe-zone placement, and adaptation/testing.

## Accessibility

- [W3C WAI: Making audio and video media accessible](https://www.w3.org/WAI/media/av/) — captions, transcripts, visual description, sign language, accessible players, and planning guidance.
- [W3C WAI: Description of visual information](https://www.w3.org/WAI/media/av/description/) — audio-description methods and the distinction between integrated, alternate, and separate described versions.

## Maintenance notes

- Re-check platform specifications, advertising guidance, newsroom standards, accessibility standards, and AI/disclosure policies when a request depends on current requirements.
- Prefer the official current page over cached PDFs when both exist.
- Add a source only if it changes an editing decision or boundary; avoid turning this into a generic film-school bibliography.
- When a new editor interview contradicts an existing stylistic default, preserve both as contextual options and improve the selection criteria rather than creating an absolute rule.
