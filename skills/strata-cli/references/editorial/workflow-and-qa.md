## Editorial artifacts

Create only what the project needs, and give each one the fields that make it usable: an
**editorial brief** (audience, promise, runtime, mode, POV, objective, constraints), a **footage
inventory** (source, date, people, content, quality, rights), a **factual chronology** (event,
source, certainty, discrepancies) that keeps real order separate from dramatic order, a
**transcript or paper edit** (source timecode, speaker, selected text, status), **selects** rated by
function, a **style grammar** and a **rhythm map**, a **rights and provenance log** (owner, licence,
territory, term), a **cut-note log** (version, timecode, symptom, request, decision) and a
**delivery matrix** (platform, runtime, ratio, fps, audio, captions, slate, deadline).

## Footage intake and discovery

**Inventory before interpretation.** Inspect folder structure, metadata, camera/card continuity,
audio channels, frame rates, resolutions, colour metadata and timecode; preserve originals and work
from managed copies, proxies or linked media; sync and group sound and multicamera material before
judging performance; identify missing, corrupt, offline, clipped, out-of-focus, unstable or
rights-unknown material; and keep generated, stock, archive, reenacted and original material
distinguishable in bins and logs.

**Log for editorial retrieval** — decisions another editor can search: who, what, where, when ·
action, topic, claim, emotion, change · strong beginning and ending points · visual proof, inserts,
reactions, room tone, wild tracks, transitions · performance quality, continuity limits, technical
issues · rights, consent, sensitivity and fact-check flags. A marker named "good" is not reusable
knowledge; "03:14 subject stops before answering layoffs question; visible hesitation" is.

**Separate evidence from interpretation.** For factual footage label each item **Observed**
(directly visible or audible), **Stated** (claimed by a participant or source), **Verified**
(checked against reliable evidence), **Inferred** (editorial interpretation) or
**Unknown/disputed**. This prevents a dramatic hypothesis silently becoming fact.

## Build passes

1. **Backbone.** Dialogue-led: a paper or radio edit with source links. Action-led: intention,
   decisive events, results, consequences. Music-led: song form and performance sync. Animation:
   dialogue, boards and essential effects timed in the story reel. Do not solve coverage, graphics,
   colour or effects before the piece has a coherent change.
2. **Structure.** Test opening promise, progression, turns, climax and ending; remove beats that
   repeat a function however attractive the material; separate necessary context from information
   that can arrive later; create contrast between sections in pace, scale, sound or POV.
3. **Performance and POV.** Track who owns each beat and whose reaction matters, compare takes in
   surrounding context, preserve thought, listening, behaviour and subtext, and keep reaction
   chronology truthful in factual work.
4. **Picture grammar.** Refine eye trace, geography, action continuity, shot scale, focal hierarchy
   and transition motifs; replace coverage that only hides edits with images that add story or
   evidence; review visual density at the intended display size.
5. **Sound.** Repair intelligibility and continuity before adding score; establish ambience and
   sound perspective; add motivated effects, pre-laps, tails, contrast and silence; shape music
   around story turns; listen without picture to catch unclear structure.
6. **Text, graphics and access.** Titles, lower thirds, source labels, captions, diagrams,
   disclaimers, CTA; resolve competition among speech, captions, graphics and picture; plan audio
   description where essential visual information is unspoken; review safe zones, reading time,
   contrast and localisation expansion.
7. **Fine cut and finishing handoff.** Refine frame-level timing once structure is stable, remove
   temporary effects with no story purpose, lock or flag shots needed for VFX, mix, grade,
   animation, licensing and fact-check, and keep the handles and turnover metadata the finishing
   workflow requires.

## Style bible

For a series, campaign or team edit, write the editorial blueprint from
[editing-director.md](../editing-director.md) (thesis, viewer promise, position on the range,
grammar, transitions, sound, text and graphics, integrity and access) once for the whole run rather
than per piece, plus the four fields a series adds: **dominant POV**, **rhythm behaviour**
(baseline, escalation, recovery, ending), **named transition exceptions and what each means**, and
**the recurring visual motif**. Do not specify a device because it is available: a useful bible says
what each device means and when it should not appear.

## Give and resolve cut notes

### Strong note anatomy

The note shape, the grouping (Must fix / Should improve / Experiments / Keep) and the rule that a
note names the viewer effect rather than a style preference are in [review.md](../review.md) §4 —
use that shape here too. Notes that prescribe style without identifying the experience ("more
dynamic", "make it pop", "speed this up") are not notes.

### Triage

| Priority | Meaning | Response |
|---|---|---|
| Must fix | Wrong fact, broken story, missing requirement, rights/access/safety issue | Resolve before approval |
| High leverage | Major clarity, emotion, pacing, or brand problem | Test the smallest structural change |
| Polish | Frame trim, mix nuance, graphic alignment, continuity | Address after structure |
| Experiment | Plausible alternative with a tradeoff | Version separately; compare in context |

When notes conflict, restate the shared outcome and compare alternatives against it; never average two incompatible ideas into a compromise without testing.

### Version comparison

Change one major hypothesis per version, label versions by the editorial question rather than only
`v12` and `v13`, watch from before the changed beat through its consequence, and keep a decision log
of reversals and rejected experiments.

## Audience and pacing evaluation

Ask viewers to answer without prompting: who wants what · what changed · what was confusing or
late · where attention dropped and what they understood by then · the strongest image, line, sound,
joke, scare, proof or benefit · what they expect next · what action they should take. Do not ask
only whether they "liked it"; preference does not diagnose structure.

When analytics exist, treat drop-offs and replays as questions, not edit commands — a replay can be
delight, confusion, density or an accidental loop, and a drop can be fulfilled intent, the wrong
audience, a technical fault or deliberate discomfort. Compare the moment against audience source,
device, placement, promise and surrounding information, and test a focused hypothesis before
generalising a convention to future videos.

## Factual integrity and provenance QA

Before approval verify that every consequential quote links to its source and surrounding context ·
translations and subtitles preserve meaning and uncertainty · dates, locations, names, titles,
statistics and on-screen documents are correct · archive and stock are labelled where viewers could
mistake their time or place · reactions and event sound are contemporaneous or disclosed · music,
grading, slow motion and effects create no materially false implication · allegations, disputes and
responses follow the applicable standard · generated or synthetically altered material is disclosed
per the publisher's policy · consent, privacy, safety and participant-impact issues have named
owners · and rights, licences, releases and restrictions appear in the final cue/provenance log.
For news the applicable newsroom policy overrides stylistic preference; for documentary keep a
review path covering ethical accountability, not only legal clearance.

## Accessibility as editorial design

**Captions and transcripts.** Caption meaningful speech and the non-speech audio needed to
understand the piece; synchronise to meaning and speaker changes and correct automatic
transcription; keep captions readable over changing backgrounds and clear of critical picture,
lower thirds and platform UI; break lines by phrase, not character count; identify speakers the
image does not; provide a transcript where the delivery context requires it.

**Audio description.** Identify essential visual information absent from dialogue and main sound —
actions, characters, scene changes, charts, on-screen text. Integrate it into narration for
training and explainer work; for narrative and observational work plan pauses or an alternate
described mix rather than crowding dialogue. If everything essential is already spoken, separate
description may not be needed — verify against the applicable standard.

**Cognitive and visual legibility.** Avoid simultaneous narration, dense captions, fast graphics and
critical action; give viewers time to read unfamiliar names, numbers, instructions, disclaimers and
CTAs; check text size, contrast, motion and localisation at the intended display size; never carry
a critical distinction in colour alone.

**Flash safety.** Avoid flashing content where possible. For web content, W3C WCAG 2.2 Success
Criterion 2.3.1 requires that content not flash more than three times in any one-second period
unless it stays below the defined general and red-flash thresholds. Analyse the final
highest-dynamic-range version at the largest expected viewing scale with a flash-analysis tool
wherever intense flashes, strobes, lightning, muzzle flashes or rapid exposure changes exist. A
warning is not a substitute for meeting an applicable threshold.

## Delivery and technical QC

Obtain the current destination specification and confirm: runtime and edit duration · frame size,
aspect ratio, pixel aspect and orientation · frame rate and scan requirements · codec, container,
bitrate or mezzanine · colour space, transfer function, range, HDR/SDR and legal levels · audio
layout, channel order, sample rate, loudness, true peak, stems and mix versions · caption format,
language, forced narrative and burn-in/sidecar · slate, bars and tone, countdown, handles, textless
elements, clean versions and naming · thumbnail, title, metadata and platform safe zones. Do not
guess a platform, broadcaster, festival or distributor spec that can be verified.

### Full-watch QC

Watch the exported deliverable, not the timeline: correct start and end with no unintended black,
freeze, flash or missing frames · sync stable throughout · no offline media, placeholders,
watermarks, temp score, guide narration or disabled effects · titles, captions, lower thirds,
credits and disclaimers spelled, timed, positioned and legal · no clipped dialogue, ambience holes,
clicks, phase problems or channel mistakes · graphics and reframes correct in every aspect-ratio
variant · colour transforms and levels matching the intended display · flash safety and
accessibility assets passing · file name, checksum, version and manifest matching.

### Variant QC

Review each deliverable independently. The common adaptation failures: text or faces hidden by
platform controls · captions duplicated by burned and sidecar versions · 16:9 reframes that break
9:16 composition or the reverse · CTA or legal copy removed by a runtime trim · music licensed for
organic use but not paid media · the wrong mix, language, date, price, product, end card or
campaign code.

## Handoff summary

At completion report: creative status and approved direction · major unresolved choices and their
audience effect · missing, offline or proposed material · factual, consent, rights, compliance and
accessibility status · finish work still required (VFX, grade, mix, online, captions, QC, exports) ·
deliverables verified and any specs still assumed. Never call a cut "final" while a known must-fix
integrity, rights, accessibility or delivery issue remains.
