## 1. Story structure for motion
- **Hook in 2-3 seconds**: motion, a face, a question, or a bold word before anything else; on social the scroll moves on otherwise.
- **Arc templates**: problem → solution → proof → CTA (explainer); setup → build → payoff (brand); hook → value → how → CTA (product); before → after (transformation); list or countdown (social).
- **Three acts in 30 seconds**: 5 s hook, 20 s body, 5 s payoff and CTA.
- **Beat sheet**: list the moments before designing frames; one beat = one idea = one visual change.
- **Shot list and storyboard**: frame count per beat decided before animating.
- **Animatic**: a timing pass with rough frames and real audio; fix rhythm before polish.
- **Pacing curve**: energy over time; draw it; avoid flat lines.
- **Crescendo**: shorten intervals and enlarge amplitude toward the climax.
- **Breath before payoff**: a still or quiet beat before the hero moment.
- **Climax reveal**: the biggest, slowest, cleanest move of the piece.
- **Callback**: reuse an early motif at the end for closure.
- **Rule of three**: three examples, three beats, three steps.
- **Repetition and variation**: establish a pattern, then break it on purpose.
- **Contrast pairs**: fast/slow, big/small, loud/quiet, dense/empty; contrast is the engine of attention.
- **Chapter markers**: color swaps, title cards, or a progressing number.
- **Progress cues**: the viewer senses where they are (a bar, a count, a recurring frame).
- **End card hold**: 2-3 s minimum with a still CTA; logos need a settled frame.
- **Seamless loop narrative**: the end is the beginning; social and ambient.
- **Split-screen narrative**: parallel stories or comparison in one frame.
- **Continuous one-shot**: a camera journey connecting all beats.
- **Personalization arc**: generic hook → the viewer's own detail → consequence → action.
- **Question and answer beats**: pose a question visually, hold, answer with the hero move.
- **Tension ladder**: each beat raises a stake slightly until release.

## 2. Pacing and editing
- **Shot length**: 1-3 s for social energy, 3-6 s for explainers, 6-10 s for premium brand pieces.
- **Cut on beat**: align cuts to downbeats; every fourth beat for calm, every beat for energy.
- **Rhythm variation**: alternate lengths; no more than three equal shots in a row unless repetition is the point.
- **Speed ramps**: slow into a reveal, fast out of it.
- **Slow motion**: for weight and beauty; 40-60% speed is usually enough.
- **Frame economy**: if a beat can lose 6 frames without losing meaning, cut them.
- **Watch at 1x, then frame-step**: rhythm lives at 1x; errors live in frames.
- **Mute test**: the story must survive without sound (autoplay is muted).
- **Thumb test**: watch on a phone at arm's length; if the hero isn't obvious, it fails.
- **Front-loading**: the most valuable information appears before the median drop-off point.
- **Micro-pacing**: inside one beat, the hierarchy of entrances creates its own rhythm.

## 3. Sound and music
- **Audio-first**: lock music and VO before animating; motion follows sound.
- **Beat grid**: mark downbeats and accents on the timeline; key events land on them.
- **Hit points**: mark every impact, swell, and transient; each gets a visual response.
- **Beat math**: frames per beat = fps × 60 ÷ BPM (25 fps at 120 BPM = 12.5 frames).
- **VO pacing**: 140-160 words per minute; text lands on the spoken word.
- **Syllable sync**: kinetic type hits syllables 1-3 frames early.
- **SFX vocabulary**: whoosh (moves), click and tick (UI and type), riser (build), impact (landings), sub drop (reveals), shimmer (glows), pop (appearances).
- **Sound per element class**: each element type gets a consistent sound family.
- **Ducking**: music drops 6-12 dB under VO; motion calms while VO speaks.
- **Silence as emphasis**: cut music for the key number or word.
- **Swells and stabs**: long crescendos pair with long moves; stabs pair with snaps.
- **Tempo to easing**: faster BPM → stronger easing and shorter durations.
- **Audio-reactive motion**: amplitude drives scale, glow, or bar height; frequency bands drive different layers.
- **Sound logo**: 1-2 s mnemonic; the motion logo ends on its last note.
- **Loopable music** for loopable motion; match loop points exactly.
- **Foley for materials**: paper, glass, metal sounds sell the material of a 2D element.
- **Spatial audio cues**: pan sound with the object's position.
- **Loudness targets**: streaming -14 LUFS, broadcast -23 LUFS; true peak under -1 dB.
- **Captions and mute-first**: design for muted autoplay; burn-in captions with the same motion rules as titles.
- **Pre-roll and tail**: a few frames of sound before the first visual and a tail after the last keep the piece from feeling clipped.

## 4. Emotional mapping (brief adjectives to motion parameters)
- **Calm, serene**: durations 800-1500 ms, sine or gentle ease, small distances, fades and slow drifts, long holds, parallax under 20%.
- **Energetic, bold**: 150-300 ms, expo easing, large distances, hard cuts on beat, overshoot on hero shapes, shake on hits.
- **Luxurious, premium**: slow asymmetric easing, blur-ins, light sweeps, tracking reveals, shallow depth of field, restrained palette, generous hold.
- **Playful, friendly**: bounce, squash and stretch, rotation on entrance, rounded shapes, springs, bright color swaps.
- **Trustworthy, corporate**: consistent durations, symmetric choreography, no overshoot, grid-aligned rests, crisp edges.
- **Precise, technical**: circ or expo snaps, stepped counters, HUD lines, monospace ticks, thin strokes, grid reveals.
- **Tense, urgent**: jitter, asymmetric timing, accelerating cuts, desaturation with one warning color, pulsing.
- **Nostalgic, warm**: grain, frame drops, warm grade, soft bounces, hand-drawn boil, light leaks.
- **Futuristic**: chromatic aberration, glows, decode text, orbit cameras, dark backgrounds with neon accents.
- **Organic, natural**: arcs everywhere, noise-driven drift, blobs and liquid, slow ease-in-out, earth palettes.
- **Human, handmade**: boil, imperfect paths, paper textures, stop-motion stepping.
- **Authoritative, news**: fast lower thirds, tickers, sharp wipes, symmetric layouts, bold condensed type.
- **Celebratory**: confetti, scale pops, light flashes, rising elements, major-key hits.
- **Serious, somber**: slow fades, dark palette, minimal motion, long holds, no sound effects.
- **Curious, discovering**: reveals through masks and windows, push-ins, rack focus, question-then-answer beats.
- **Confident, direct**: cut-ins on beat, big type, few moves, no decoration.
- **Tone sliders for any brief**: energy (slow↔fast), weight (light↔heavy), precision (organic↔mechanical), warmth (cool↔warm), density (sparse↔rich), playfulness (serious↔playful). Set the six sliders first, then everything else follows.
